# Brand & Design Direction Notes

## Logo (fixed asset)
- Circular badge/seal monogram: interlocking J/X/R forming an hourglass-like shape
- Colors: true black, a strong medium blue, white
- Reads as official/institutional — closer to a military unit crest or a seal than a typical contractor logo
- Treat black, this blue, and white as the fixed palette anchors — do not replace

## Palette direction
- Keep: black, the logo's blue, white
- Add: warm off-white/parchment as the primary background (instead of stark white) to soften the badge's high-contrast starkness across large page areas
- Add: charcoal/graphite as a secondary neutral for body text and UI chrome
- Accent (sparingly, for CTAs/highlights only): consider a muted brass/gold or desaturated brick-red — avoid a literal red-white-blue tricolor treatment, which would compete with the badge rather than extend it

## Typography direction
- Headlines: confident serif or humanist sans — institutional trust, not construction-flyer boldness
- Body: clean grotesk, generous line height
- Avoid heavy slab/condensed "job site" lettering styles

## Motif
- Lean into the badge/seal concept already established by the logo (precision, insignia, credentialing)
- Thin structural/blueprint rule lines, grid marks — precision and engineering cues
- Avoid stars/stripes or flag iconography — the badge does the "official" work already; literal patriotic decoration would be redundant and undercut the restraint

## Rationale
The company's actual credibility comes from substance: a combat veteran founder with a structural engineering degree, named federal contracting officers vouching for the work, zero safety incidents, zero terminated contracts. Design should express discipline and precision through restraint, not through decorative patriotism — letting the content (and the existing badge-like logo) carry the "trustworthy/official" signal.
